The Culture & Music of Cabo Verde

Cabo Verde is a nation that breathes music. From the aching melancholy of morna to the accordion-driven joy of funaná, this small archipelago has produced a remarkably rich musical world. Let the videos be your guide.

About the video links: Clicking a card opens the corresponding YouTube video (official channels where possible) in a new tab. The videos are published and managed by their respective rights holders; this site only links to them. If a video won't play, it may have been removed or region-restricted.

🎶Morna — the saudade of the Atlantic

Cabo Verde's signature genre. Over slow, lyrical melodies, morna sings of saudade — a bittersweet longing for absent loved ones and homeland. Often compared to Portuguese fado, it was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2019, and is traditionally played on clarinet, violin, guitar and cavaquinho.

👑Cesária Évora — the Barefoot Diva

The national singer (1941–2011) who brought Cabo Verde to the world. Known as the "Barefoot Diva" for performing without shoes, she was born in Mindelo on São Vicente and won a Grammy. Her voice is a symbol of Cabo Verde itself.

🪘Beyond morna: a world of genres

Morna is only the beginning. Each island and region carries music with its own distinct character.

Funaná
Funaná — accordion frenzy
e.g. Ferro Gaita, Bulimundo
From Santiago: fast, joyful dance music driven by the gaita (accordion) and ferrinho (an iron bar scraped with a knife).
Search on YouTube ↗
Coladeira
Coladeira — light and witty
e.g. Os Tubarões, Ildo Lobo
A livelier, more rhythmic dance music that split from morna in the 1930s, often with satirical lyrics.
Search on YouTube ↗
Batuko
Batuko — the women's soulful rhythm
Batukadeiras (women performers)
One of Santiago's oldest traditions: women strike bundled cloth and sing in call-and-response — the most African, most powerful sound of the islands.
Search on YouTube ↗
